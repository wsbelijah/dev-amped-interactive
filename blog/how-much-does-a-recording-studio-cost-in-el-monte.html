How Much Does a Recording Studio Cost in El Monte? Real Prices, Explained

By AMPED UP AUDIO · November 1, 2026

In El Monte, at AMPED UP AUDIO, a recording studio costs $40 an hour — with an engineer included. Not $40 for an empty room and extra for the person who runs it. The engineer is behind the desk the whole session. Mixing is flat, not hourly: $100 for a 2-track mix, $150 for a full stem mix. That's the whole price list, and it's published on the services page before you ever make contact.

Most studios don't answer this question that plainly, which is why artists end up guessing. Here's how the money actually works — here, and anywhere else you're comparing.

The AMPED UP AUDIO price list — all of it

Recording: $40/hour — engineer included

Every recording session at AMPED is $40 an hour with an engineer. Mixing stays flat: $100 for a 2-track mix, $150 for a full stem mix.

  • Recording: $40/hour, engineer included. Track vocals, instruments, or a full session with the engineer running the room.
  • 2-Track Mix: $100 flat. You send one stereo two-track, you get back a finished record.
  • Full Stem Mix: $150 flat. You send your separated stems, and every element gets balanced into the final record.
  • Deposit: $40, via Zelle. That's your first hour. It locks the session once the studio confirms your time.

No packages. No bundles. No “call for pricing.”

What a real session actually totals

Hourly rates are hard to picture, so run the math on the sessions artists actually book:

  • 2-hour recording session: 2 × $40 = $80 total. Your $40 deposit covers the first hour; $40 is due for the second.
  • 3-hour recording session: 3 × $40 = $120 total.
  • 4-hour recording session: 4 × $40 = $160 total.
  • Record it and mix it (2-track): a 3-hour session ($120) plus a 2-track mix ($100) = $220 from first take to finished record.
  • Record it and mix it (stems): a 3-hour session ($120) plus a full stem mix ($150) = $270, with every element balanced separately.

One more number worth knowing before you book anywhere: sessions here come with a 20-minute grace period, then a $20 late fee, and late arrival doesn't extend your session time. Showing up on time is literally worth money.

Why other studios quote you differently

Studios that don't publish a rate aren't all hiding something — but the quote you're given can mean very different things. Before you compare two prices, ask these:

  • Is an engineer included, or is it room-only? A lower hourly number for an empty room is a different product than a session with an engineer behind the desk. At AMPED, the engineer is included in the $40 — that's the comparison that matters.
  • Is mixing hourly or flat? Hourly mixing can keep growing while a song gets finished. Flat mixing ($100 / $150 here) means you know the total before the work starts.
  • What's the deposit, and what does it lock? A deposit should lock a confirmed time, not just hold a maybe. Here, booking starts as a request, the studio confirms your time with you directly, and the $40 deposit locks it.
  • What are the hours? A cheap rate at a studio that closes before you get off work isn't cheap — it's unusable. AMPED runs late on purpose: weeknights to 2 AM, with Friday through Sunday adding daytime hours (9 AM–3 PM) plus 9 PM–2 AM.
