2-Track Mix vs. Full Stem Mix: What's the Difference and Which Do You Need?

By AMPED UP AUDIO · November 3, 2026

Recording and mixing are two different steps. Recording is capturing the performance — at AMPED UP AUDIO in El Monte, that is $40/hour · with engineer included. Mixing is what happens after: balancing the vocals and the music so they sit together as one finished song. The question most artists run into is not whether to mix, but which kind of mix their files actually call for. That comes down to one simple thing: how your beat arrives.

AMPED UP AUDIO has been operating since October 2025, and this is one of the most common points of confusion before a session. The names sound technical, but the difference is straightforward once you look at the files themselves — and choosing the right one up front saves confusion about what can and cannot be adjusted later.

What a 2-track mix is

Recording: $40/hour · with engineer

Every recording session at AMPED is $40/hour with engineer included. Mixing stays flat: $100 for a 2-track mix, $150 for a full stem mix.

A 2-track mix starts with a single stereo instrumental. “Two-track” means the beat is one finished file with two channels — left and right — and everything inside it (drums, bass, melody, and any other elements) is already combined into that one file.

  • What the engineer can adjust: your vocals — their level, tone, and placement against the beat — and the overall level and tone of the stereo instrumental as a whole.
  • What the engineer cannot adjust separately: one element inside the beat on its own. If the bass feels loud, it cannot be turned down without affecting everything else in that same file, because it does not exist as a separate file.
  • When it fits: you bought, downloaded, or were sent the beat as a single file, and that is the version you have. That is a normal, common situation, and a 2-track mix is built for exactly it.

At AMPED UP AUDIO, a 2-track mix is $100. It is not a lesser version of mixing — it is the mix that matches a song recorded over a single stereo instrumental.

What a full stem mix is

A full stem mix starts with the song separated into its parts. Those separated files are called stems — for example, the drums as one file, the bass as another, the melody as another, and the vocals kept separate as well. Instead of one combined instrumental, the engineer receives the pieces.

  • What the engineer can adjust: each stem on its own — its level and tone relative to everything else — before the pieces are brought together. If one element is crowding the vocal, that element alone can be brought down.
  • What it requires from you: the stems themselves. That usually means getting them from whoever made the beat, or exporting them from the project the beat was made in. If you do not have them and cannot get them, there is nothing to separate after the fact.
  • When it fits: you have the stems, or you are recording multiple parts yourself and each part is captured separately, so the whole song exists in pieces rather than as one instrumental file.

At AMPED UP AUDIO, a full stem mix is $150. The higher flat price reflects the extra elements being balanced individually — not a promise that every song needs it.

How to decide which one you need

Start with your files, not with the price. Look at what you actually have: if your instrumental is one file, you are set up for a 2-track mix. If your song exists as separated stems, you are set up for a full stem mix. A simple check before you book recording time — which is $40/hr with an engineer at AMPED UP AUDIO — is to ask whoever provided the beat one question: can I get the stems? Their answer tells you which mix is even possible.

A few other honest considerations help. If the beat already sounds balanced on its own and your main goal is getting your vocals recorded well and placed cleanly over it, a 2-track mix covers that. If you already know you want specific elements in the music brought up, brought down, or reshaped around your voice, that level of control only exists when the elements arrive separated. And if you are still writing or arranging, decide that first — mixing, of either kind, works best on a finished performance, not on a song that is still changing.

Questions artists ask

What is a 2-track mix?

A 2-track mix is when your vocals are recorded and mixed over a single stereo instrumental — the beat arrives as one finished file with two channels, left and right. The engineer balances your voice against that stereo file, but cannot turn the individual instruments inside the beat up or down separately. At AMPED UP AUDIO, a 2-track mix is $100.

What is a full stem mix?

A full stem mix is when the elements of the song arrive separated — for example the drums, bass, melody, and vocals as their own files, called stems. The engineer can balance and adjust each element on its own before bringing them together. At AMPED UP AUDIO, a full stem mix is $150.

Which mix do I need for my song?

If you only have the beat as a single stereo file, a 2-track mix is the option that matches your files. If you have the separated stems, or you can get them from whoever made the beat, a full stem mix gives the engineer more control over the balance. Neither choice is about quality on its own — it is about how much of the song arrives separated.
