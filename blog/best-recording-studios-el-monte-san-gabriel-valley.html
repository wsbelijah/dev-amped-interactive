Best Recording Studios in El Monte & the San Gabriel Valley: 6 Options Compared

By AMPED UP AUDIO · October 30, 2026

If you're looking for the best recording studio in El Monte, the honest answer is that it depends on what you're recording — and this list will tell you which room fits which kind of artist. In El Monte itself, AMPED UP AUDIO records at $40 an hour with an engineer behind the desk. Around the San Gabriel Valley, you've also got solid options in Montebello, Alhambra, Pasadena, Pico Rivera, and Whittier — here's how they actually compare.

One thing before the list: this article lives on AMPED UP AUDIO's own site, so read it that way. Every studio below is real, every one is described the way it describes itself, and AMPED is on the list because it's the El Monte option — not because we hid who wrote this.

2. Mixwizards Studios — Montebello

Mixwizards is a recording studio in Montebello, a few minutes southwest of El Monte, running sessions by appointment. Its listing shows long hours — 9 AM to 2 AM daily — and, like most serious rooms, it asks for a deposit to book. If you're based in Montebello and want to stay on that side of the valley, it's a legitimate local option worth a call to compare session details.

3. SGV Sound — Alhambra

SGV Sound in Alhambra works across analog and digital recording, and lists mixing, mastering, and an hourly rehearsal room alongside recording. That rehearsal angle matters: if you're a band that needs to tighten up live before you track — or you want rehearsal and recording under one roof — SGV Sound is structured for that in a way a vocals-focused room isn't.

4. Summit Rehearsal and Recording Studios — Pasadena

Summit in Pasadena leads with rehearsal — six rehearsal rooms with backline — and runs a recording studio offering production, engineering, mixing, demos, and voiceover work. For bands in the Pasadena area who rehearse weekly and record occasionally, having both in one building is the draw. It's the furthest option on this list from El Monte, so factor the drive into a late session.

5. Idle Tuesdays Recording Studio — Pico Rivera

Idle Tuesdays in Pico Rivera is a nonprofit recording studio built around positive music and influencing culture through it. That mission is the filter: if your music fits what they're building, it's a genuinely different kind of room — community first, not a commercial hourly studio. If you're shopping purely on rate and schedule, compare it on those terms too, but know the mission is the point there.

6. The Hott Boxxx Studio — Whittier

The Hott Boxxx Studio in Whittier is a recording studio that also works in post-production. For artists on the Whittier side of the SGV who need recording with post work in the same place, it's the closest fit on this list. As with any studio here, confirm current session pricing and what's included directly with them before you book.

How to actually choose

  • Price in the open: AMPED publishes its full rates — $40/hour recording with an engineer included, $100 for a 2-track mix, $150 for a full stem mix — so you can budget before you ever make contact. Where a studio doesn't publish rates, get the session price, what's included, and the deposit in writing before you commit.
  • Engineer or room rental? Some studios rent you a room; some put an engineer behind the desk. Those are different products at similar-looking prices. At AMPED, the $40/hour always includes the engineer. Know which one you're paying for anywhere else.
  • Your sound: a room that lives in your genre will get your vocal right faster than a room that records everything. Ask what they record most.
  • The hours that fit your life: if you work days, a studio that closes at 6 PM is a studio you can't use. Late hours are a real feature, not a gimmick.
  • The drive home at 1 AM: El Monte, Montebello, Alhambra, Pico Rivera, Pasadena, Whittier — pick a room you can actually get to and from, repeatedly, while you're making a project.

How much does a recording studio cost in El Monte?

At AMPED UP AUDIO in El Monte, recording is $40 an hour with an engineer included. Mixing is flat-rate: $100 for a 2-track mix and $150 for a full stem mix. Other SGV studios set their own pricing — some publish it, some quote per session.

What is the difference between a 2-track mix and a full stem mix?

A 2-track mix works from one stereo file — your beat and vocals already combined — and finishes it as a record. A full stem mix starts from your separated elements (drums, bass, vocals, and so on as individual tracks), so the engineer can balance every element against the others. Stems give more control; a 2-track is faster and costs less.
